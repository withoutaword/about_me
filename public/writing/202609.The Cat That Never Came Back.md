---
title: The Cat That Never Came Back
date: 2026-09-01
summary: A childhood memory of a cat that once found its way home—and the small act of care I thought about but never carried out.
tags:

---

My family once had a cat. It lived with us for more than ten years.

I no longer remember exactly when we first took it in. I only remember that it was always there when I was a child, and that nobody looked after it in any special way. Our circumstances were modest. Keeping a cat was different then: there was no cat food, and we rarely prepared anything especially for it. It ate whatever the family happened to have.

Sometimes there was fish. Sometimes there was not.

When there was nothing, it went out to find food for itself.

Eventually, it began stealing fish from our neighbor.

After this happened several times, the neighbor complained. My father decided it could not continue. He put the cat in a bag, carried it far away, and left it there.

I was still in middle school. There was not much I could do.

But I did think of something.

I thought about going to the market and buying fish for it. If it had enough to eat, perhaps it would stop stealing from the neighbor.

I had very little money then, and the market was far from home. I considered it several times, but in the end I never went.

The cat was taken away.

We assumed it would never return.

More than a month later, it suddenly appeared at home.

I no longer remember the details of that day. I only remember how astonished everyone was.

Nobody knew how it had found its way back.

It had been carried away inside a bag, unable to see the route, and more than a month had passed. We did not know where it had been, how far it had walked, or what it had eaten along the way.

It had simply come home.

Many years later, I still think about that journey.

I do not know whether a cat understands “home” as a person does. I do not know whether it returned because the place was familiar or because of an animal's instinct to find its territory.

I only know that it came back.

After its return, life remained the same. Our circumstances had not changed. The cat still went outside, and it still stole the neighbor's fish.

It happened several more times.

My father decided to take it away again.

He put it in a bag as before.

This time, he carried it even farther.

It never came back.

More than ten years have passed, and I still think of it occasionally.

I wonder whether it tried to find the way home again after the second journey. I wonder where it went and how it lived.

I will never know.

I do not want to invent an ending for it.

I only know that, back then, I had thought about buying it a fish.

I have remembered that thought ever since.

Later, I found work and began earning my own money.

Once, at a market, I stood in front of a fish stall. There were crucian carp, sea bass, yellow croaker, and other fish, with prices written on pieces of cardboard.

I looked at them for a while.

**They were not expensive.**

Suddenly, I remembered myself many years before, standing at home and thinking about going to the market to buy a fish. I had counted the money in my pocket, thought about how far away the market was, and finally stayed where I was.

The feeling is like a tree whose roots reach somewhere very deep. Most of the time, you cannot see them, but they are always there.

There is nothing I can do now for the cat we had then.

I can no longer even remember exactly what it looked like.

Strangely, I have never forgotten its first return.

After being carried away in a bag, it disappeared for more than a month.

Then one day, it was home again.

The second time, it never returned.

— Bruce W, September 2026
